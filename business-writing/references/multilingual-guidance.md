# Multilingual Guidance

Use this reference when writing, translating, or editing communication in a language where natural phrasing or regional conventions materially affect the result.

The skill is not limited to the languages listed here.

## General principle

Translate the communication, not the individual words.

Preserve:

- intent;
- hierarchy of information;
- degree of warmth;
- directness;
- formality;
- technical terminology;
- metrics and their meaning;
- the relationship between sender and recipient.

Rewrite syntax when needed to make the result natural in the target language.

Do not deliberately retain grammar errors or non-native phrasing merely to preserve the source author's language level.

At the same time, avoid making a simple message substantially more elaborate in translation.

Do not change the meaning of numbers, units, percentages, dates, targets, or comparisons while translating.

Adapt decimal separators, date conventions, currency presentation, and similar formatting when appropriate to the target language or locale without changing the underlying values.

## English

Favor plain international business English.

Avoid unnecessary corporate jargon.

Use contractions when appropriate to the relationship and tone.

Match spelling conventions to the context when known.

Internal English communication can be compact and direct.

## Spanish

Prefer natural, internationally understandable business Spanish unless a regional variety is clearly relevant.

Keep professional communication warm but concise.

Avoid overly ceremonial wording.

Use "Saludos" or "Saludos cordiales" when appropriate, but do not hard-code a closing.

Adapt pronouns and level of formality to the recipient and region.

## Dutch

Prefer natural Dutch syntax instead of directly translating English sentence structure.

Business communication can be concise and direct while remaining friendly.

Avoid making short practical emails more formal than necessary.

When the user supplies understandable but non-native Dutch, correct it naturally rather than preserving literal constructions.

## Portuguese

Use natural business Portuguese and adapt to Brazilian or European Portuguese when the context identifies the target variety.

Do not mix regional conventions.

## Italian

Favor clear contemporary business Italian over bureaucratic phrasing.

Maintain warmth where the source communication is relationship-oriented.

## French

Prefer clear professional French and avoid excessive formality unless the context requires it.

Match pronoun choice and greetings to the relationship and locale.

## Simplified Mandarin

When Simplified Chinese is requested, prioritize clear, natural contemporary Mandarin.

Preserve the business intent rather than translating sentence structure literally.

Use appropriate professional politeness without making the message more ceremonial than the original.

When a wording choice depends strongly on hierarchy, relationship, or regional business convention and that context is missing, identify the ambiguity rather than inventing it.

## Mixed-language terminology

Preserve product names, company terminology, acronyms, system names, and established internal labels when translating them would cause confusion.

Translate generic surrounding language naturally.

If a term has an established localized version supplied by the user or organization, use that version consistently.
