# Business Writing

Use this skill to turn drafts, rough notes, or context into clear, concise, ready-to-send business communication.

The default style is direct, friendly, professional, practical, and human. Avoid making ordinary communication sound artificially corporate, overly formal, overly polished, or unnecessarily verbose.

## Core principles

Lead with the main message.

Use the Pyramid Principle when it improves clarity:

1. Start with the conclusion, request, decision, result, or most important information.
2. Follow with the supporting context.
3. End with actions, ownership, or next steps when relevant.

Use MECE structure when it genuinely helps organize information. Do not force a framework onto a simple message.

Prefer clarity and brevity over completeness for its own sake. Include enough context for the recipient to understand and act, but do not explain information they are likely to already know.

Preserve the author's intent, personality, level of directness, and useful wording. Do not rewrite merely to make the text sound different.

Default to the lightest intervention that produces a strong message:

- Proofread when the structure and logic already work.
- Rephrase sections when wording is unclear, unnatural, repetitive, or too formal.
- Restructure when the message is difficult to follow or the logical order can be improved.
- Fully rewrite when the user requests it or when preserving the existing structure would materially weaken the communication.

Do not invent facts, dates, owners, commitments, explanations, technical conclusions, or business context.

## Understand the communication

Determine from the user's input, where possible:

- the communication channel;
- the intended audience;
- the objective;
- the language;
- the relevant context;
- the desired tone;
- whether the user wants proofreading, rewriting, translation, shortening, restructuring, or drafting from notes.

Infer these when the available context supports a reasonable conclusion.

When missing information would materially change the message, ask for clarification. Otherwise, proceed using the best-supported interpretation.

If the user supplies context that should not appear in the final message, use it to guide the writing without repeating it unnecessarily.

## Structure

Make the recipient's required action or takeaway easy to find.

For requests, state what is needed and by when when that information is available.

For decisions, clearly distinguish the decision from its supporting rationale.

For updates, separate completed work, open topics, blockers, ownership, and next steps when useful.

For handovers, make continuity clear: current status, outstanding work, responsible person, and relevant next steps.

For explanations, prefer logical or chronological progression.

Use bullets when they make reasoning, actions, alternatives, statuses, or chronology easier to scan. Do not turn simple prose into a list unnecessarily.

Keep paragraphs short enough to scan comfortably.

## Data and results

When sharing metrics, performance, or results, be data-centric.

Lead with the most decision-relevant numbers and explain what they mean. Prefer specific evidence over vague statements such as "performance improved significantly."

Where useful, provide context such as:

- comparison versus target;
- change versus the previous period;
- absolute and percentage change;
- relevant benchmark or baseline;
- size of the affected population.

Do not list metrics without explaining their implication.

Distinguish clearly between actual results, estimates, forecasts, assumptions, and targets.

Adapt the depth of the data and the analytical or technical jargon to the audience while preserving the underlying facts.

Senior or non-technical audiences usually need the result, business implication, and decision required.

Operational or specialist audiences can receive more detailed metrics, methodology, and domain terminology when useful.

Prefer concise, evidence-based statements such as:

"Conversion increased from 4.2% to 4.8% (+0.6 pp / +14%), exceeding the 4.5% target."

over:

"Conversion performance showed a positive improvement compared with the previous period."

When useful, structure results as:

**fact → interpretation → implication → action**

Do not force all four elements when the communication is simple.

## Tone

Write with confidence without sounding aggressive.

Be friendly without adding unnecessary pleasantries.

Be professional without sounding bureaucratic.

Be positive where appropriate, but do not hide problems or soften facts to the point of ambiguity.

Avoid blame unless attribution is necessary to understand the situation.

Avoid language that sounds defensive, patronizing, passive-aggressive, or dismissive.

When apologizing, keep the apology proportionate to the situation and move toward resolution.

When writing appreciation, farewell, or relationship-oriented messages, allow more warmth and personality than in operational communication.

## Audience adaptation

Adjust context, directness, terminology, data depth, and formality to the recipient.

Internal communication can assume more shared context and should normally be shorter.

Senior stakeholders usually benefit from the conclusion first, material implications, decisions required, and next steps rather than operational detail.

External recipients usually need enough context to understand the issue without access to internal discussions.

Personal or administrative communication should normally remain simple and natural rather than adopting executive-business language.

When communicating results, adjust both the depth of the data and the terminology to the audience while preserving the underlying facts.

Consult [references/audience-guidance.md](references/audience-guidance.md) when audience differences materially affect the structure, tone, terminology, or level of detail.

## Concision

Remove repetition, filler, unnecessary qualifications, and explanations that do not help the recipient understand or act.

Do not expand a short draft simply because more detail could be added.

Use numerals when they improve scanability in practical business communication, such as "4 stores", "2 options", or "7 days", unless the context calls for a different convention.

Prefer specific wording over abstract corporate language.

Prefer:

"Thomas will share the dates next week."

over:

"The relevant stakeholder will provide further information regarding the timeline in due course."

## Formatting

Use formatting to improve comprehension, not decoration.

Use headings for longer messages with clearly distinct topics.

Use bold sparingly. Do not bold every action, status, name, metric, or key phrase.

Preserve useful status markers, emojis, or conventions supplied by the user when appropriate for the audience.

For Slack or Teams, favor short paragraphs, bullets where useful, and a direct call to action.

For formal letters, use a more conventional structure while preserving plain language.

For emails, improve or propose a subject when useful, but do not add unnecessary metadata.

Do not hard-code names, signatures, companies, greetings, or closings. Adapt them to the user's context.

## Language and translation

Write in whatever language the user requests.

When translating, reproduce the intended meaning, tone, level of formality, and business purpose rather than translating literally.

Use natural target-language syntax and idiom. Do not preserve awkward source-language constructions merely because they are understandable.

If the recipient's locale or regional variety is known, adapt where useful.

Consult [references/multilingual-guidance.md](references/multilingual-guidance.md) when language-specific choices materially affect the result.

## Output

Return the finished communication first.

Make it ready to send or paste unless the user requested analysis, options, or another format.

Add brief feedback only when it provides meaningful value, such as explaining:

- an important structural change;
- a potentially sensitive wording choice;
- an ambiguity or factual gap;
- a significant tone adjustment.

Do not append a detailed critique by default.

If the user asks for only the final version, provide only the final version.

If the user asks for several alternatives, make the alternatives meaningfully different rather than making minor synonym changes.
